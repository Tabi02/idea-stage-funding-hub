Funding scams to avoid

The rule is absolute: real investors never ask you for money before funds are in your account. If an offer matches one of these patterns, walk away — the fee is the product, and the investment does not exist.

Documented traps

  • Fake investors demanding a “due diligence fee”. A slick “VC” promises a large investment, then demands a processing, due-diligence or valuation-report fee before releasing funds. Often quoted in the tens of thousands of rupees.
  • Fake family offices. Cold messages from people posing as a family office, ending in a processing or documentation fee — sometimes escalating to luring founders to in-person meetings abroad. Delete, do not engage, and never travel on the strength of a cold approach from an unverifiable source.
  • Fake application fees for real programs. Some programs have never charged an application fee — for example, the Tony Elumelu Foundation Entrepreneurship Programme is free, on TEFConnect only. Anyone requesting a fee to “apply” or “register” for it is running a fraud in its name.
  • “Accelerators” that charge you to pitch. A recurring wave of fake accelerators promises a pitch in front of top VCs at an exclusive demo day, in exchange for an upfront application, coaching or sponsorship fee. Red flags: VC logos used without permission, fabricated testimonials, high-pressure deadlines, and no transparency about which investors actually attend.
  • Celebrity-headliner funding festivals. A funding event that sells tickets on celebrity names is selling tickets, not funding. If no promised guest or investor appears, that is the pattern, not bad luck.

Legitimate programs that do charge

Two real, long-running programs in the directory charge founders, disclosed here and on their cards so there are no surprises: Founder Institute (a course fee plus a warrant, and it invests nothing) and Gritworks NZ (an upfront fee). Paying a disclosed course fee to a real program is a choice; paying an “application fee” to anyone else is almost certainly a scam. TinySeed deducts a fee only after acceptance, from its investment.

Quick scam check

  • Application fee requested? Almost certainly a scam — except the disclosed cases above.
  • “Guaranteed funding”? No real fund guarantees outcomes before diligence.
  • Old listing, dead program? Some programs shut down years ago while stale listings still circulate — always re-check the official page.
