Avoid: scams & fake funding programs

Documented traps that imitate idea-stage funding. If an offer matches one of these patterns, walk away.

Avoid · documented scam

World Startup Convention (India, 2023)

Billed as the “world's biggest funding festival” with fake headliners — Elon Musk, Sundar Pichai, Masayoshi Son, Nitin Gadkari. Tickets cost ₹6,000–₹8,000+ (reports go up to ₹25,000; sponsors paid lakhs).

No promised guest or investor appeared. 500+ attendees protested, and Gautam Budh Nagar police booked the organisers. A funding event that sells tickets on celebrity names is selling tickets, not funding.

Avoid · fee scam

Fake investors demanding a “due diligence fee”

A slick “VC” or “angel network” promises a large investment — say ₹2 crore — then demands ₹45,000–₹50,000 as a “due diligence fee”, “processing fee” or a “valuation report from an authorised vendor” before releasing funds.

The rule is absolute: real investors never ask you for money before funds are in your account. The fee is the product; the investment does not exist.

Avoid · impersonation

“Drexel Morgan” fake family office

Cold DMs from people posing as representatives of a family office called Drexel Morgan. The script ends at a “processing” or documentation fee — and can escalate to luring founders to in-person meetings abroad.

Delete, do not engage, and never travel or pay on the strength of a cold approach from an unverifiable “family office”.

Avoid · impersonation

Fake Tony Elumelu Foundation application fees

The Tony Elumelu Foundation Entrepreneurship Programme (listed in the directory) has never charged an application fee — applications are free, on TEFConnect only.

Anyone requesting payment to “apply”, “register” or “unlock” a TEF grant is running a fraud in the foundation's name.

Avoid · pay-to-pitch

“Accelerators” that charge you to pitch

A recurring wave — reported across Latin America and beyond — of fake accelerators promising a pitch in front of top VCs at an exclusive demo day, in exchange for an upfront “application”, “coaching” or “sponsorship” fee.

Red flags: VC logos used without permission, fabricated testimonials, high-pressure payment deadlines, and no transparency about which investors actually attend or what diligence happens.

Not scams · but not free

Legitimate programs that do charge

Two real, long-running programs in this directory charge founders — disclosed here and on their cards so there are no surprises: Founder Institute (course fee of $399–$699 plus a 2.5% warrant, and it invests nothing) and Gritworks NZ (NZD $1,000 upfront).

Paying a disclosed course fee to a real program is a choice; paying an “application fee” to anyone else is a scam.

Stay safe: fees & dead programs

No legit program charges you to apply

Across the 100+ programs researched here, almost none charges applicants anything. Legitimate accelerators and funds make money from equity or not at all — never from application fees.

The disclosed exceptions are flagged on their cards: Founder Institute charges a course fee ($399–$699 plus a 2.5% warrant) and Gritworks NZ charges NZD $1,000 upfront — both real programs, but you pay before any funding. TinySeed deducts a fee only after acceptance, from its investment. Anyone else demanding an “application”, “due diligence” or “processing” fee is running a scam — see the list above.

Pioneer.app is shut down

Pioneer's own site states it stopped making new investments in 2024, yet aggregator listings still show it as open. Those listings are stale.

Anyone soliciting applications or fees in Pioneer's name is working from stale listings at best. Don't confuse it with Pioneer Fund, a separate, live YC-alumni fund listed in the watch list.

How to read this directory

“Official site” vs “second-hand”

Every card is badged. Official site means the details were read on the program's own website. Second-hand means they come from news, aggregators or blogs — treat the numbers as a lead and verify on the official page before relying on them. A red low verification badge means the sourcing was thin: amounts, dates — sometimes even whether the program still runs — need re-checking before you rely on them.

Where a deadline or intake couldn't be confirmed at all, the card says so plainly instead of guessing.

Idea-stage funding, explained

The five routes to money before you have a product — and what each one costs you.

Accelerators

Fixed-term programs (usually ~3 months) that invest a standard amount for equity and end in a demo day. Typical idea-stage deal: $120K–$500K for about 7–10%. Examples: Y Combinator, Techstars, Antler, Flat6Labs.

Pre-seed funds

Venture funds that write the first cheque, often on a SAFE, before any traction exists. Checks run from ~$100K to ~$1M (Afore Alpha). Many take rolling pitches or warm introductions instead of cohort applications.

Grants & fellowships

Equity-free money from philanthropies, universities and governments — nothing to repay and no shares given up. Ranges from $1,000 micro-grants (1517 Medici) to the $250,000 Thiel Fellowship and India's ₹50 lakh BIRAC BIG.

Company builders

Programs that start one step earlier than accelerators: they recruit talented individuals, help them find a co-founder and an idea, then fund the company that forms. Examples: Entrepreneur First, Zinc, EWOR, Antler's residencies.

Pitch competitions

Prize money and investor exposure with no application fee: Hello Tomorrow (€200,000 grand prize), the Startup World Cup ($1M investment prize) and the Hult Prize ($1M, students). You win visibility even when you don't win cash.

Government schemes

Public money for the earliest stage, usually equity-free and routed through incubators: India's NIDHI-PRAYAS and MeitY TIDE 2.0, Germany's EXIST, France's Bpifrance grants, Israel's Tnufa and Start-Up Chile's Build track.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers on getting funded at the idea stage, drawn from the programs listed above.

Can you get startup funding with just an idea and no product?

Yes. A specific class of programs funds founders before any product, revenue or registered company exists: company builders such as Entrepreneur First and Antler, pre-seed funds such as Y Combinator and Afore Alpha, equity-free grant programs such as Emergent Ventures, and government schemes such as India's NIDHI-PRAYAS and BIRAC BIG. This directory tracks 129 such programs worldwide, 68 of them open now.

What is idea-stage funding?

Idea-stage funding is money raised before a startup has a product, revenue or traction — earlier than pre-seed. It comes as accelerator investments (typically $100K–$500K for roughly 7–10% equity), equity-free grants and fellowships ($1,000–$250,000), pitch-competition prizes, and government grants. Funders are betting on the founder and the idea rather than on metrics.

Which accelerators accept founders at the idea stage?

Y Combinator (Winter 2027 batch, $500,000 standard deal, applications close 2 Nov 2026), Antler, Techstars, Flat6Labs and Founder Institute all accept idea-stage founders. Company builders Entrepreneur First, Zinc and EWOR go further and accept people who do not even have an idea or a co-founder yet.

Are there startup grants that take no equity?

Yes — 57 of the 129 programs in this directory take no equity. Examples: Emergent Ventures (equity-free grants, rolling), the Thiel Fellowship ($250,000 over two years), 1517 Fund's Medici Project micro-grants ($1,000+), Germany's EXIST stipend (up to €3,000 per month for 12 months), Switzerland's Venture Kick (up to CHF 150K+), Start-Up Chile's Build track (~$15,000), and India's NIDHI-PRAYAS (up to ₹10 lakh) and BIRAC BIG (up to ₹50 lakh).

How much money can a founder raise at the idea stage?

Idea-stage checks in this directory range from $1,000 micro-grants (1517 Medici Project) to about $1M (Afore Alpha's standard pre-seed deal; South Park Commons' Founder Fellowship at roughly $1M per team). The most common band is $100K–$500K from accelerators and pre-seed funds — Y Combinator's standard deal is $500,000 — while equity-free grants typically run from $5,000 to $100,000.

What idea-stage funding is available for founders in India?

India-based founders at the idea stage can apply to NIDHI-PRAYAS (DST grant up to ₹10 lakh for individual innovators, no startup required), BIRAC BIG (up to ₹50 lakh for biotech ideas), NIDHI-EIR fellowships (₹10K–₹30K per month), MeitY TIDE 2.0 (₹4–7 lakh for idea validation), state schemes such as Startin UP in Uttar Pradesh, and Antler India's ARC program (up to $500,000 at concept stage). Global programs such as Y Combinator, Emergent Ventures (which has an India-focused track) and South Park Commons (Bangalore) also accept Indian founders.

Do legitimate funding programs charge an application fee?

Almost never. Across the 100+ programs researched for this directory, legitimate accelerators, funds and grant programs do not charge application, due-diligence or processing fees — anyone demanding one is almost certainly running a scam (see the watchlist above). The disclosed exceptions are Founder Institute (a course fee of $399–$699 plus a 2.5% warrant, and it invests nothing) and Gritworks NZ (NZD $1,000 upfront); both are real programs that state the fee openly.

What are the next idea-stage funding deadlines?

As of 18 October 2026: Y Combinator Winter 2027 closes 2 Nov 2026 (8pm PT); Techstars programs in Anywhere, Boston, Chicago, London and New York close 18 Nov 2026; the Hello Tomorrow Global Challenge closes 30 Nov 2026; NEXT AI (Canada) closes 9 Dec 2026. Many other programs — Emergent Ventures, Antler, Afore Alpha, Entrepreneur First — accept applications on a rolling basis. See the deadline calendar for the full list.

Before you apply

  • Re-check the deadline on the official page — windows close early when cohorts fill.
  • Read the “catch” on each card: relocation, in-person residencies and team requirements rule out more founders than idea quality does.
  • Prefer a warm intro where a fund has no public application portal.
  • Keep one tight 1,500-word version of your idea — several grant programs ask for exactly that.

Quick scam check

Application fee requested? Almost certainly a scam — the only fee-chargers in this directory (Founder Institute, Gritworks NZ) are disclosed on their listings.

“Guaranteed funding”? No real fund guarantees outcomes before diligence.

Old listing, dead program? Pioneer.app shut down in 2024 — stale listings still circulate.
