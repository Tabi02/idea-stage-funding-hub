How to apply for idea-stage funding

Idea-stage funding is money raised before a startup has a product, revenue or traction — earlier than pre-seed. Funders are betting on the founder and the idea rather than on metrics. These are the routes, and what each one costs you.

Accelerators

Fixed-term programs, usually about three months, that invest a standard amount for equity and end in a demo day. A typical idea-stage deal is in the $100K–$500K range for roughly 7–10%. Examples: Y Combinator, Techstars, Antler, Flat6Labs.

Pre-seed funds

Venture funds that write the first cheque, often on a SAFE, before any traction exists. Checks run from around $100K to about $1M. Many take rolling pitches or warm introductions instead of cohort applications.

Grants and fellowships

Equity-free money from philanthropies, universities and governments — nothing to repay and no shares given up. Ranges from $1,000 micro-grants to the $250,000 Thiel Fellowship and India's ₹50 lakh BIRAC BIG.

Company builders

Programs that start one step earlier than accelerators: they recruit talented individuals, help them find a co-founder and an idea, then fund the company that forms. Examples: Entrepreneur First, Zinc, EWOR, Antler's residencies.

Pitch competitions

Prize money and investor exposure with no application fee: Hello Tomorrow, the Startup World Cup and the Hult Prize are examples. You win visibility even when you do not win cash.

Government schemes

Public money for the earliest stage, usually equity-free and routed through incubators: India's NIDHI-PRAYAS and MeitY TIDE 2.0, Germany's EXIST, France's Bpifrance grants, Israel's Tnufa and Start-Up Chile's Build track.

Best first moves

  • Keep one tight 1,500-word version of your idea — several grant programs ask for exactly that.
  • Read the “catch” on each card before applying: relocation, in-person residencies and team requirements rule out more founders than idea quality does.
  • Re-check every deadline on the official page — the directory is a starting point, not the final word.
  • Prefer rolling, equity-free routes first if you are solo and pre-product: Emergent Ventures and similar grant programs accept applications on a rolling basis.
  • Never pay an application fee — see scams to avoid.
